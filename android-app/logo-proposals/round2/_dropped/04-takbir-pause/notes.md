# 04 Takbir Pause («وقفة التكبير»)

**Idea.** Two hands raised for the opening takbir stand side by side as the two bars of a pause sign: when you say "Allahu akbar", the world is put on pause.

**Silence.** Two upright bars with a straight channel between them are the most familiar "pause" mark. Nothing is slashed or crossed.

**Prayer.** Each bar is a hand: four fingers held together (three short cuts, middle finger tallest), a thumb beside the index finger leaning slightly toward the head, and a sleeve cuff. The sleeves run out of the bottom of the frame and flare a little, so the hands belong to one person standing below the frame, not to two floating palms.

**Palette.** Zitouna olive gradient #6A8A30 to #33471A, ivory hands #FFF8EC, sand sleeves #E8DCC0.

**Small sizes.** At 48px the fingers, thumbs and cuff gap all show, so it reads as hands first. At 24px two light bars with a straight gap are left: a pause. The themed icon is the same silhouette, with the finger cuts and thumb notch as real notches and the cuff as a true gap.

**Variants** (`_var/`, `gen.py --vars`). Thumbs sticking out sideways (a-protrude) closed the gap and lost the pause. Short cuff blocks (b, c) looked like white gloves. Arc-cut sleeves (c-smile, h-clip) looked like a bowl. Madder red looked like a video player. Red sleeves looked like Christmas.

**Risks.** Raised palms can still read as "hands up" to someone who does not know the takbir. The pause meaning has to be understood; it is less literal than a speaker. The sleeves deliberately run past the 66dp circle (16% of the ink). It looks Tunisian only through the olive colour.
