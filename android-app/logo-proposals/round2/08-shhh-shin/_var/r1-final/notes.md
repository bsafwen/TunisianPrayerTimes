# 08 «ششش» Shhh

**Idea.** One bold sheen (ش), the letter Tunisians type as «ششش» for "shh", with a small crescent above it: "hush, it is prayer time".

**Silence.** The three rounded teeth step down in reading order (right to left) and run out into a long kashida. The kashida thins steadily to a soft round tip (2.7dp), so the "shhh" is written as a ring dying away into stillness. The baseline sags slightly and lifts at the end, so it never looks like a flatline. For people who don't read Arabic, the crescent also echoes the familiar "do not disturb" moon.

**Prayer.** The crescent sits in the letter's empty top-left space, well clear of it, with its horns turned toward the dots. Nothing is crossed out or dimmed.

**Palette.** Cream `#FFF3E2` on a Nabeul terracotta gradient `#CC6A3E` to `#A0462A`. Themed icon: the letter, dots and crescent in one flat colour.

**Small sizes.** At 48px the letter, its three rhombic dots and the crescent are all crisp. At 24px the gaps between the teeth close, but the shape still reads as a sheen with dots and a tail, and the crescent stays a crescent.

**Variants (`_var/`).** `c-round`, `g-smooth` and `i-letter` are the letter alone: purer, but the top left is empty and there is no prayer cue. `d`, `h` and `j` are crescent studies; `j` is too thin at 24px, and the final sits between `d` and `j`. `e-blue` (Sidi Bou Said `#1B5E9E` on cream) and `f-olive` (`#4E5A28` on cream) are fresh, but the cream disc gets lost on light wallpapers. Terracotta has more presence. The crescent version won because it balances the composition and carries the prayer cue.

**Risks.** The «ششش» joke only lands for Arabic readers. The crescent could suggest a sleep app. At 24px the teeth merge.
