Launcher icons for «أوقات الصلاة تونس»

The current icon is a generic dome between round Ottoman minarets. Its gold crescent never renders, because the path has zero area, and it has no monochrome layer for themed icons. Every proposal below is a full adaptive icon (background, foreground and monochrome layers), checked against the 66 dp safe zone and shown the way launchers crop it.

Silence and calm

Two answers to “the logo should show the app’s main feature, silencing the phone during prayer”. The first hides the silence in the shape of the day’s arc. The second says it through mood: one still light in a quiet niche. Each was picked from several candidates by a craft judge and a meaning judge, then refined.

S

الأفق الهادئ

The Quiet Horizon

Proposal 02's square Maghrebi minaret under the gold arc of the day, with one change: the arc is a volume line. It rises full from the horizon at sunrise, thins steadily as the day goes on, and stops with a small round end a short, deliberate pause before the sun at Asr. The day's noise fades to zero just as the prayer arrives.

shows silence

Picked from 5 candidates; both judges ranked it first. Nothing religious is slashed or muted: the silence lives in the sky arc, which thins like a volume line and stops before the sun. Rejected along the way: a dome drawn as a silent bell (bells are disliked in the hadith tradition, and the adhan was chosen over the bell), and a dark phone slab with an arch, which read as a headstone. The silence is subtle by design and needs one sentence: «ضجيج النهار يخفت حتى يتلاشى لحظة يحين وقت الصلاة».

How it reads, symbolism, risks

How silence reads

  • First reading: a square minaret, the sun and a gold arc over a dusk horizon. It reads as prayer time, in Tunisia. At small sizes the arc also reads as a gold crescent around the minaret, which strengthens the prayer reading.
  • Second reading: the arc is a line that gets quieter. It is full where it stands on the ground, thins to a thread over the minaret, and stops before it reaches the sun. It is the shape of a volume level turned down to zero. The empty stretch of sky between its end and the sun is the silence: the moment the ringer goes off.
  • The sentence that makes it click: «The day's noise fades to nothing just as prayer time comes. That is the moment the app silences your phone.» In Arabic: «ضجيج النهار يخفت حتى يتلاشى لحظة يحين وقت الصلاة: عندها يُسكت التطبيق هاتفك».
  • When presenting it, call it "a volume line that fades to zero at prayer time". Do not use music terms (decrescendo, hairpin): Tunisian users will not know them. The folder name keeps the old working title only.
  • The silence is in the shape of the arc, not in a symbol laid on top. Nothing is slashed, crossed or muted. The minaret and the sun are untouched.
  • Possible support for the idea (not used in the icon): a reviewer recalled the hadith "arihna biha ya Bilal" ("give us rest through it, Bilal", said of the prayer), cited as Sunan Abi Dawud 4985. It could not be checked here (sunnah.com refused the request). Verify the number and text on sunnah.com before quoting it anywhere.

Symbolism

  • Square minaret (from 02): a straight 20 dp shaft running off the tile, a cornice with two pointed corner merlons, a slim lantern with a pointed opening, a gold cupola, rod and ball (jamour), a pointed window and the paired Maghrebi arches. It is the Zitouna/Kairouan form, not an Ottoman pencil. The jamour rod is now 1.6 dp wide (was 1 dp), so the ball sits on it instead of floating.
  • Gold arc: the sun's path from the sunrise horizon (left, east) towards sunset. It is 7.5 dp wide where it stands on the horizon, about 3.1 dp over the finial and 1.2 dp at its end, in one smooth fade with no steps (profile power 0.85, slightly fuller than linear). 90% of the extra width grows inward, so the outer curve stays close to 02's and the weight sits nearer the tower.
  • Flat foot on the horizon (y 72): the day and its sound start from the ground at full strength. The flat cut also stops the arc from reading as a plain crescent at large sizes.
  • Round end: the line keeps a minimum width of 1.2 dp and closes with a round cap at the angle of travel, 46° up the descending side. It ends on purpose instead of vanishing, so the pause before the sun has the same length at every size.
  • The pause: 4.0 dp of empty sky between the round end and the sun's edge. It is the moment the phone goes quiet.
  • Sun at Asr (26° up the descending side, as in 02), radius 6 dp (was 5.6): the prayer time. Nothing continues past it, which leaves the lower-right sky empty: the window of silence.
  • Teal dusk sky and darker ground (from 02): the «أفق» mood of the TV app in the phone palette.

Palette

  • #00453A: tile top and bottom edges (never darker than #003A31, so the tile holds on dark wallpapers).
  • #004D40: sky halfway down (phone primary teal).
  • #0A7363: horizon glow just above the horizon. The gold foot stands on it at 3.07:1, and the gold reaches 5.2:1 to 5.8:1 higher in the sky.
  • #004A3D: ground just under the horizon, fading to #00453A.
  • #FFF8F0: cream minaret (5.5:1 to 10.4:1 against the sky).
  • #E2B845: icon gold for the arc, the sun, the cupola and the jamour (the brand #D4AF37 raised for small sizes, shared with 02).
  • The foreground uses 2 colours.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • Ink: 26.9% of the visible 72 dp square (high-contrast ink at 3:1 or more: 26.7%). Bounding box x 26.8-84.0, y 24.0-90. The arc's farthest point is 30.2 dp from the centre and the sun's edge 32.3 dp. The 5.0% of ink outside the 66 dp circle is the tower's foot running off the tile on purpose. No ink falls outside the 72 dp square. The high-contrast centroid moved from x 50.9 to 52.0 (centre 54): the composition is still a little left-weighted, which the sun on the right answers.
  • 48 px (preview cell): tower, gold finial, arc and sun all read. The fade is plain to see: thick on the left, a thread at about half past one, a clear gap, then the sun. The 1.2 dp end is under one pixel here but still shows as a faint line, so the gap stays the same.
  • On a real phone 48 dp is about 130-170 physical px (48 dp at 2.6x to 3.5x), so the 72 dp visible square is drawn at about 2 px per dp. There the 1.2 dp end is 2 to 3 px wide and the fade is far easier to see than in the 48 px preview cell.
  • 24 px: a cream tower inside a gold crescent, with a gold dot. Prayer reads clearly. The fade softens into "a crescent that thins on the right", which the brief allows.
  • Themed icon: one flat #000000 on transparent with no partial alpha. The tower's openings are evenodd holes and the cupola is cut 0.8 dp above the lantern, as in 02. The arc never touches the tower or the sun, so the tint shows three parts: the fading arc, the sun disc and the minaret. In one tint, the arc and the dot also recall the moon of the system's bedtime / Do-Not-Disturb mode: a quiet, accidental hint of silence, not the main idea.

Changes in this round (judges' notes)

  • Weight: foot 8.8 to 7.5 dp, inward growth raised to 90% of the width, sun 5.6 to 6 dp. Centroid x 50.9 to 52.0, ink 27.0% to 26.9%. A thinner foot (7.0 dp, centroid 52.3) was tried and dropped: the fade got weaker at 48 px for little gain.
  • Tip: minimum width 1.2 dp with a round cap and a fixed 4 dp pause before the sun (was a point that vanished below 64 px, leaving a 2.6 dp gap that grew with smaller sizes).
  • Jamour rod 1 to 1.6 dp in both the colour and the monochrome layers.
  • The duplicate vertex at the arc's tip is gone. The generator now drops repeated vertices in every polygon.
  • Arc foot vs horizon on launchers that move the layers apart: risk accepted knowingly. Stopping the foot 0.5 dp above the horizon was rendered and dropped. At large sizes (Play Store, 96 px and up) it shows as a sliver of light teal under the foot, which looks like a near miss, and it would only hide shifts under 0.5 dp. Independent layer motion is brief and rare (launch and drag animations), and during it the foot simply sinks into or lifts off the horizon for a moment, like the tower crossing the horizon.

Variants tried (gen.py and variants.py keep them)

  • Stepped radial bars: the most literal "volume going down", but they read as a dotted dial or loading spinner at 48 px and turned to noise at 24 px. Dropped.
  • Fade then rise again after the sun (the ringer coming back): at a glance it read as 02 again and hid the fade. Dropped.
  • Round thick foot: read as a blob or comet head. Replaced by the flat foot.
  • Pointed tip (round 1): the end disappeared below 64 px and the gap changed with the size. Replaced by the round end.

Risks

  • Reading direction: an Arabic reader scanning right to left meets the thin end first and could read the arc as growing out from the sun. The sun anchors it (the arc is the day already travelled), and the explanation sentence fixes it, but the fade is not self-evident.
  • Crescent: the thick left half can read first as a gold crescent with a disc beside it, which a reviewer may link to the crescent and star. It is not the flag: there is no red, the disc sits outside the curve, and it is the sun, not a five-pointed star.
  • Comet or swoosh: a tapering arc next to a disc can suggest a comet, a sports swoosh or a weather or sunrise app at a glance. With the minaret present it never reads as a sleep or DND app.
  • The silence is subtle by design. Without the one sentence most people will see "a minaret under a gold arc with the sun", not a muted phone. The owner asked for a discreet cue, but it does depend on being told once.
  • Still slightly left-weighted (centroid x 52.0, not 54).
  • Inherited from 02: the tower can suggest a lighthouse or a rook, and the sun is fixed at Asr.
  • The arc is a 160-point polyline with one arc command for the cap. It is fine for a VectorDrawable but cannot be hand-edited like 02's arcs: change it through gen.py.
C

قنديل المحراب

The Lamp in the Niche

A bold Kairouan horseshoe mihrab in pale sand, with a deep teal niche. One gold Maghrebi lantern hangs on a single chain inside it, and a soft warm glow lights the half-dome around it. It shows the mosque at night: everything is quiet, and only the lamp is lit. The image is the niche and the lamp of the Light Verse (an-Nur 24:35), so its meaning holds all year, not only in Ramadan.

shows calm

Picked from 4 candidates (still water, olive-branch crescent, prayer rug, lamp). The craft judge ranked it first; the meaning judge preferred the still-water minaret, whose themed icon however reads as a gun sight. The niche holding a lamp is the image of the Light Verse (an-Nur 24:35), whose lamp is lit from a blessed olive tree, the zaytuna of Zitouna. Risk: a lantern can suggest Ramadan, or a riad or spa sign outside the Maghreb. It shows prayer and stillness, not time.

How it reads, symbolism, risks

How calm reads

  • There is one light and one still object, centred in a symmetric arch. Nothing moves, points or competes for attention.
  • The light fades slowly from warm gold to deep teal, so the niche has no hard edge. It feels like a mosque between prayers.
  • The lantern hangs straight down. A plumb line is the opposite of a phone that rings and shakes. This is the app's promise: while you stand in the niche, nothing disturbs you. The calm makes that point, so the icon needs no mute symbol.
  • The palette is warm and closely matched (sand, honey gold, teal). There is no white-on-black flash and no saturated accent.

Symbolism

  • The niche and the lamp: the Light Verse (an-Nur 24:35) compares God's light to a niche (مشكاة) that holds a lamp (مصباح). The verse goes on to say the lamp is lit from a blessed olive tree (زيتونة), and the olive tree is the name of the Zitouna mosque in Tunis. This gives the icon a scriptural reason and a Tunisian one. The verse number is 24:35 in the app's own Qaloun index ("later Madani" numbering, checked in android-app/app/src/main/assets/quran/index.json: surah 24, ayah 35), and also in Hafs. None of the verse's text appears in the icon.
  • Horseshoe arch on columns with capitals: the mihrab of the Great Mosque of Kairouan (and of Zitouna). The arch curves back inward below its widest point, 12 dp under the arch centre. This is a Maghrebi form, not an Ottoman or Mughal one. The mihrab shows the direction of prayer, which also points to the qibla compass.
  • The lamp in the mihrab: the lamp that hangs in the niche on prayer rugs (sajjada). It stands for prayer, for the night prayers (Isha, and Fajr with the app's wake alarms) and for light in the heart.
  • The lit window with a horseshoe top: the lantern's glass panel repeats the shape of the niche. It is a mihrab inside the mihrab, the light inside the place of prayer.
  • The deep niche and its radial glow: depth, stillness and the inward turn of prayer. It is made only from a radial gradient (no blur), so it stays VectorDrawable-safe.

Palette

  • #004D40: tile edge (brand teal)
  • #00695C: tile centre, in a radial gradient behind the arch (light brand teal)
  • #F8EEDB: arch, columns, sill and the lantern's lit window (pale sand, a warmer step from brand cream #FFF8F0)
  • #EDC659: lantern and chain (honey gold, a little lighter than icon gold #E2B845 so it stands out from its own halo)
  • Niche: a radial gradient centred on the lantern at (54, 51.5), r 24. The stops are #D8B65C (0) → #9A8746 (0.20) → #3F6A50 (0.46) → #0F4A3D (0.80) → #033D34 (1, deep teal). The edge stays clear of black, so the niche reads as teal, not as a hole.
  • There are 3 foreground colours: sand, gold and the niche gradient.
  • Contrast: the frame is 8.5:1 against the tile edge and 5.75:1 against its centre. The lantern edge against the halo is 3.7–4.0:1 at the dome, the bands and the finial, and 2.5:1 at its thinnest point beside the window. In round 1 these figures were 3.0–3.3:1 and 1.9:1.

Geometry

The arch has an outer radius of 24 dp and an inner radius of 18 dp around (54, 48), so the frame band is 6 dp. The sill is 4.5 dp tall and exactly as wide as the columns (x 34.88–73.12). The ink spans x 30–78 and y 24–80.5 (48 × 56.5 dp), and none of it falls outside the 66 dp safe circle. The base corners are 0.3 dp inside the safe circle and 3.3 dp inside a circular launcher mask. The arch top has 6 dp of margin in round masks. Foreground ink covers 43.4% of the visible 72 dp square, and the monochrome layer covers 22.4%. The lantern is 17.6 dp wide and 31 dp tall (y 34.2–65.2). The chain is 3 dp wide. The window is 9.1 × 9.3 dp, and each gold border around it is at least 2.26 dp.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • 48 px: you can read a sand horseshoe niche, a gold lantern with a crisp teardrop outline and a pointed dome, and a cream window. The halo is a warm haze that stops short of the lantern's edge.
  • 36 px: the lantern stays a distinct gold shape on darker teal, and its window still shows as a light inside it.
  • 24 px: the details merge into a sand arch with a gold drop of light and a pale dot in a dark niche. It still reads as an arch with a light in it, on both light and dark wallpapers.
  • Monochrome: the frame and sill are solid, and the niche is an evenodd hole, so the launcher's tint shows through as the niche. The chain and lantern are solid, and the window is cut out of the lantern. It reads as a lantern hanging in an arch on both themed light and themed dark, at 48 px and at 24 px. The glow is lost (no partial alpha), so the themed icon is crisper and less atmospheric than the colour one.

Round 2 changes (judges' notes)

  • The halo is tighter: the stops moved to 0.20 #9A8746 and 0.46 #3F6A50. The lantern's sides now sit on darker teal, so its outline is crisper at 36–48 px.
  • The window is 10% larger, so the light inside the lamp still shows at 36 px. The lantern is 6% wider so the gold borders stay at least 2.26 dp. The window keeps the frame's sand tone, because #FFF8F0 would add a fourth foreground colour for only 1.55:1 against 1.42:1.
  • The sill is cut to the width of the columns, and the whole group moves down 1 dp. The arch is better centred (bounding-box centre at y 52.25, was 51.25), and it reads more like a framed niche than a gate on a plinth.
  • The chain is 3 dp wide (was 2.6 dp). This adds a little themed-icon ink.
  • Two ideas were tried and declined (in _var/R2c, _var/R2d):
  • A sill running the full arch width (48 dp) only fits inside the safe circle if it sits 3 dp higher, which shortens the columns. At 48 px it read as a door on a threshold, more Moroccan and less like a niche.
  • A rectangular alfiz cannot surround a 48 dp arch inside the 66 dp circle. Even with the arch scaled to 78%, the frame's corners sat 37.6 dp from the centre, and 4.1% of the ink fell outside the safe circle. Circular masks cut its corners, the smaller arch lost presence, and the result read as a door in a door frame.

Variants tried (in `_var/`)

  • Round 1 lanterns:
  • A: a shield-shaped window, which read as a badge or an acorn.
  • B: the glass mosque lamp of the prayer rugs. At icon size it read as a vase, and its flame read as a water drop.
  • B with a three-chain hanger: this added clutter.
  • F, the lantern: kept. Of the frame and niche options tried with it, the sand frame and the deep-teal niche edge were the calmest pair.
  • Round 2: R2a, a column-width sill; R2b, an ivory #FFF8F0 frame and window, a little colder for almost no gain in contrast; R2c, a full-width sill; R2d, an alfiz; R3b and R3c, a fuller lantern and a lower glow centre; R3d, the 3 dp chain, which is final. R0 is round 1's final design.
  • python gen.py regenerates the final design, and the header of gen.py lists the settings for each variant.

Risks

  • The Maghrebi lantern (fanous) is strongly linked with Ramadan, so at first glance some users may see a Ramadan app rather than a daily prayer app. The Light Verse gives a reason that holds all year, but the pitch has to say so. The icon cannot show it.
  • Outside the Maghreb, a horseshoe arch with a lantern is also the familiar mark of Moroccan riads, restaurants and hammam spas. The column-width sill makes it read more like a niche, but the risk remains.
  • The calm depends on the halo. In the themed icon the halo disappears, and what is left is a plain lantern in an arch: clear, but less peaceful.
  • The window reads mainly by hue (1.42:1 against the gold). It is bigger now, but at 24 px it is only a pale dot.
  • The themed icon has 22.4% ink, just above the 22% floor. The base corners are only 0.3 dp inside the safe circle, which is legal but leaves no room to move the group lower.
  • The icon shows the mosque and stillness, not time. Unlike the mihrab-clock proposal, nothing says "prayer *times*". The link to "my phone won't disturb me" goes through the mood (serenity), not through a symbol of silence.

First round: ten proposals

01

صومعة الزيتونة

Zitouna Minaret

The square minaret of the Ez-Zitouna Mosque in the Tunis medina, drawn as one bold cream tower on the app's teal and capped with a gold cupola and jamour finial. A square Maghrebi minaret says "Tunisia" before any text does, which the current round Ottoman-style minarets never did.

critics’ pick

In both critics' top three. It is the only proposal that says both Tunisia and prayer at a glance. At 24 px it can drift toward a castle or a chess rook.

Symbolism, small sizes, risks

Symbolism

  • Square shaft (26dp wide): the Maliki/Maghrebi minaret of Zitouna, the Kasbah mosque and Kairouan, the opposite of the round "pencil" minaret.
  • Three negative-space lozenges (one whole, two halved by the tower edges): the sebka lozenge net carved on the Zitouna faces. The halves suggest that the net carries on past the edges.
  • Pointed horseshoe arch at the foot: the Almohad and Hafsid arch, built from two true circular arcs that swell past the jambs. It also reads as the mosque door.
  • Projecting band with four pointed merlons (two on each side of the lantern): the crenellated parapet. The stepped Almohad merlon is reduced to its pyramidal point so it survives at small sizes and doesn't look like a castle's square crenels.
  • Slim lantern (about 1/3 of the shaft width) with a small pointed window: the jamour lantern the call is given from.
  • Gold cupola with two stacked brass spheres: the jamour finial and the only accent colour, which marks the top of the icon.
  • Soft radial glow behind the lantern: light around the minaret at the hour of the call. It is only a background tone, never a shape.

Palette

  • #004D40: background edge (brand teal)
  • #00594D: background mid-tone
  • #00796B: glow centre behind the lantern
  • #FFF8F0: tower (brand cream)
  • #D4AF37: cupola and finial (brand gold)
  • The foreground uses 2 colours.

Why it beats the current icon

  • It is recognisably Tunisian. Square tower, sebka lozenges, horseshoe arch and pointed merlons all come from the Tunis medina, where the current icon has generic round minarets and an onion-like dome.
  • It is one motif filling the safe circle: 62.5dp tall × 29dp wide, against small scattered pieces now.
  • No broken geometry. The current crescent path has zero area and never renders.
  • It has a real monochrome layer. Every detail is a true cut-out (evenodd holes) or a separate silhouette, so nothing depends on colour contrast.
  • It is VectorDrawable-clean: 1 rect and 5 paths, one radial gradient, no strokes, every path value with at most 2 decimals.
  • 0% of the ink falls outside the 66dp safe circle. The base corners sit at radius 32.7.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • 48 px: the silhouette, the four merlon points, the central lozenge, the door arch and the gold cap all read. The half-lozenges and the lantern window shrink to accents.
  • 24 px: a cream square tower with a gold tip and a notched crown. The lozenges become specks, but the tower is still clear.
  • Themed (monochrome): the silhouette is the same as the colour icon. The lozenges, door and lantern window are holes, so the tint shows them. The gold cupola merges with the lantern, but it still reads as a cap because it is 0.9dp wider than the lantern on each side.

Risks

  • A crenellated tower can be read at a glance as a castle, a chess rook or a lighthouse. The lantern, cupola, pointed merlons and lozenges reduce this but don't remove it.
  • It abstracts what makes Zitouna itself unique (the two-tone ochre stone, the full sebka net), so many people will read "a Maghrebi minaret" rather than Zitouna specifically.
  • The shaft is chunkier than the real slender tower, a trade-off for presence at 48 px.
  • The finial spheres are only 3-4dp and vanish below about 36 px.
  • The gold cupola is a stylisation: the real lantern dome is not gilded.
  • A tall, narrow motif leaves plain teal on both sides of the mask.
  • Minaret icons are common among prayer apps, although the square Maghrebi form is rare.
02

صومعة الأفق

Minaret on the Horizon

A square Maghrebi minaret (صومعة) rises in cream from the ground, framed by the gold arc of the sun's day. The arc's two feet rest on a glowing teal horizon, and the sun sits on the arc's descending side at about Asr. Prayer (the minaret), Tunisia (its square Zitouna/Kairouan form) and the time of day (the sun on its path) read as one emblem: the phone version of the TV app's «أفق».

added after review

This replaces the first «أفق» draft, a sun path over a horizon bar that both critics read as a weather icon. It puts the square minaret, the sun's path at Asr and the TV app's dusk horizon in one mark, so it says Tunisia, prayer and time of day together. The tower runs off the bottom edge on purpose. It shares its tower details with 01.

Symbolism, small sizes, risks

Symbolism

  • Square shaft, 20 dp wide with straight sides, running off the bottom of the tile: the Maliki minaret of Ez-Zitouna and Kairouan, square instead of the round Ottoman "pencil". It is not tapered and has no door at its foot, so it doesn't read as a lighthouse or a castle keep.
  • Projecting cornice with two pointed corner merlons: the crenellated parapet of Tunisian minarets. The stepped Maghrebi merlon is reduced to a point, so it doesn't read as the square crenels of a rook.
  • Slim lantern (8.6 dp, 43% of the shaft) with a pointed opening: the lantern the call is given from.
  • Jamour finial in gold: a cupola, then a thin rod, then a small ball. It is the tower's only gold and ties the minaret to the sun. The rod lifts the ball off the dome, so the top no longer reads as a ball resting on a half-dome (a pawn or an avatar head).
  • Shaft openings: a pointed-arch window under the cornice and, lower down, a pair of pointed arches side by side (the paired arch of Maghrebi minarets such as the Kasbah mosque in Tunis). The pair replaces the earlier lone sebka lozenge, so this tower no longer repeats proposal 01's lozenge motif.
  • Gold arc: the sun's path from sunrise (left foot) to sunset (right foot). It is taller than it is wide, so it also echoes the horseshoe arch of the Kairouan arcades, the arch of the TV app's «أفق» identity.
  • Gold sun at 26° up the descending side: 43% of the way from sunset back to noon. In Tunis at the equinox, Asr comes about 2 h 40 before the end of a 6 h afternoon (about 44%), so the sun stands at Asr, a prayer time on the day's path. It is cut out of the arc with 2.5 dp gaps on both sides, and the arc carries on past it to the sunset foot, so the day reads as a path and not as a progress bar. No five-prayer dots: at 48 px they wouldn't be countable next to a tower.
  • Background: a teal dusk sky, darkest at the top, brightening to a glow just above the horizon (y 72), then a hard step down to darker teal ground. It has the mood of the TV banner's Maghrib sky in the phone palette.

Palette

  • #00453A: tile top and bottom edges. That is 1.52:1 against a #1B1F1E dark wallpaper, and lighter than #003A31, so the tile never goes near-black.
  • #004D40: sky halfway between the tile top and the horizon (the phone app's primary teal).
  • #0A7363: horizon glow just above the horizon, darkened from #0E7A69 so the gold arc feet reach 3.07:1 (the 3:1 guideline for graphics).
  • #004A3D: ground right under the horizon, a 1.78:1 step down from the glow, fading to #00453A.
  • #FFF8F0: cream minaret (the brand cream). 9.3:1 on #004D40 and 5.5:1 on the glow.
  • #E2B845: icon gold for the arc, the sun and the jamour. It is the brand gold #D4AF37 raised for small sizes, shared with proposals 02 and 09. 5.2:1 on #004D40, 5.8:1 on #00453A, 3.07:1 on the glow. The brand #D4AF37 would only reach 2.74:1 on the glow, and fixing that would need a glow as dark as #076B5B, which flattens the horizon step to 1.59:1. Recommendation: adopt #E2B845 as the icon gold for the whole family, including 01.
  • The foreground uses 2 colours.

Why it beats the current icon

  • It is Tunisian. A square minaret with pointed merlons, a lantern, a jamour, a pointed arch and a paired arch replaces the round Ottoman pencil minarets and the pointed dome.
  • It says prayer and time, not only "mosque": the minaret stands for the prayer, and the sun at Asr on its arc above a horizon stands for the time of day.
  • It fills the tile. Ink covers about 29% of the visible 72 dp square. The arc spans 58 dp wide and reaches y 22. The minaret runs 59 dp from the finial (y 30.9) to the tile edge, and the visual weight is centred at x 54.2, y 56.5.
  • Every shape renders. The foreground has 2 paths with no strokes, fill-rule evenodd only for the tower's openings, and at most 2 decimals. The background is two plain rects (sky gradient down to y 72, ground gradient from y 72), so the hard horizon needs no pair of gradient stops at one offset, which imports more safely into Android Studio. No opening crosses the horizon seam: the generator asserts that every cut-out ends at least 1 dp above it (the paired arches end at y 70) or starts 1 dp below it.
  • It ships a real monochrome layer, which the current icon lacks.
  • It ties the phone app to the TV app's «أفق» redesign (horizon, dusk glow, horseshoe arch) and keeps the phone's teal and gold.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • 48 px (0.67 px per dp): the tower (13 px wide), the gold top, the arc (3 px thick) and the sun (7.5 px across) all read. The window shows as one opening and the paired arches as two small slots below it. The merlons show as two small points. Gaps: finial to the arc's inner edge 4.1 dp (2.7 px), sun to each arc end 2.5 dp, sun to the nearest merlon 6.8 dp. The lantern has 2.8 dp walls; the window and the paired arches are separated by 2.6 dp of wall and each arch has 5.3 dp of wall to the shaft edge.
  • 24 px: a cream tower with a gold tip inside a gold arch, with the sun as a dot on its right shoulder. The openings become specks and the rod disappears, so the top reads as a dome with a dot, but the silhouette is still recognisable.
  • Themed icon: the same shapes in one flat colour, with no alpha. The tower's openings are real evenodd holes. The sun is separated from the arc by real gaps, not by tone. The arc never touches the tower, so the tint shows three parts: arch, sun and minaret. The cupola is cut 0.8 dp above the lantern in this layer only, so the tint keeps a separate lantern and dome instead of one bullet-shaped tip (the gap shows as a thin line at 96 px; at 48 px it is half a pixel and nearly closes).
  • No ground cue in the themed layer: short plinths under the arc feet were tried and read as serifs at 48 px, and a continuous horizon bar would cross the tower and form a cross in a single tint. The arc feet end in round caps at y 69.
  • Safe zone: everything meaningful sits inside the 66 dp circle. The sun's outer edge is 31.9 dp from the centre and the arc's top 32 dp. The 4.7% of ink outside the circle is the tower's foot below y 85, which runs off the tile on purpose. No ink falls outside the 72 dp square. The sun at 26° also sits lower than the top-right corner where launchers draw the notification dot.

Risks

  • At large sizes, the sun above the short sunset-side stub of the arc can read as the dot of a letter «i» (a smaller gap under the sun was tried and made it worse). The tower inside the arch can also be read at a glance as a keyhole or a badge.
  • The jamour rod is 1 dp wide, under the 2.5 dp detail guideline. It renders at 96 px and up; at 48 px it is a soft sub-pixel neck, and the ball then sits as a dot over the dome.
  • A white tower with a domed lantern can still suggest a lighthouse, and pointed merlons a rook. Paired arches over a single window are also found on Romanesque bell towers; the merlons and the jamour argue for a minaret.
  • The arc's feet are in the foreground and the horizon is in the background. Launchers that move the two layers separately (parallax, press animations) can shift the feet a little off the horizon.
  • The arc is taller than a real sun path, so the tower fits under it. The sun's position is fixed at Asr: the icon can't follow the real next prayer.
  • No room for the full jamour (three balls and a crescent) under the arc: the finial is a cupola, a rod and one ball.
  • On masks smaller than the 66 dp circle, the tower's foot is cut. That is harmless, but the tower then looks cropped rather than grounded.
  • Composition (b) was tried and dropped: a deep-teal minaret silhouette against a gold-rose dusk glow, with a half sun on the horizon. At 48 px its top vanished into the dark sky, the silhouette read as a castle, and its monochrome layer was weak.
  • Minaret icons are common among prayer apps, although the square Maghrebi form is rare.

Mihrab of Time

The front of the Kairouan mihrab (a horseshoe arch on two columns with capitals, standing on a base) drawn in ivory on teal, with a deep niche behind it. Its ribbed half-dome doubles as the top half of a clock face: tapered ribs fan out from a point low in the niche like hour lines, and two gold hands, set to 3:00, start from that same point.

critics’ pick

In both critics' top three, and the craft critic's first pick: it has the most presence in the set and survives 24 px. It may read as a mantel clock. Refined: the ribs now cite the 24-rib dome over the Kairouan mihrab bay. The mihrab's own half-dome is painted wood, not a shell.

Symbolism, small sizes, risks

Symbolism

  • Horseshoe arch: the curve runs past its widest point and returns inward (about 3.6 dp on the outside and 5 dp on the inside of each side) before it lands on the capitals. This is the Kairouan / Maghrebi arch, not an Ottoman or Mughal outline.
  • Columns with capitals: the Kairouan mihrab is framed by two marble columns with capitals. Here each capital is 1.5 dp wider than its shaft on both sides, so the columns read as columns and the shape reads as a building rather than a keyhole or a clock case. The base is a plain step that grounds the shape.
  • Ribbed half-dome: 4 tapered ribs and a short 12 o'clock mark fan out from the hub and widen from 2.5 dp to 5 dp where they meet the arch rim. The ribs recall the 24-rib dome over the mihrab bay of the Great Mosque of Kairouan. They also act as hour lines on a dial and as dawn rays, and the fan recalls the sundial in the mosque's courtyard, which was used to tell prayer times.
  • Gold hands at 3:00 (minute hand on 12, hour hand on 3): about the time of Asr in Tunis in winter (roughly 14:45 to 15:20 from December to January). The hands stand for the official prayer times, which are the app's core job.
  • Deep niche gradient: the depth of the prayer niche, which marks the direction of prayer (this also hints at the qibla compass).

Palette

  • #004D40: background edge (brand teal)
  • #00695C: background glow behind the arch (brand teal, light)
  • #FFF8F0: arch, capitals, columns, base and ribs (brand cream)
  • #00342B → #001C17: niche hollow, a vertical gradient (deep teal)
  • #D4AF37: clock hands and hub (brand gold)

Why it beats the current icon

  • It is specifically Tunisian. The horseshoe arch on columns with capitals comes from the Kairouan mihrab, and the ribbed half-dome recalls the ribbed dome over the Kairouan mihrab bay. The current icon uses generic round Ottoman pencil minarets and an onion-like dome.
  • It says "prayer + time" in one shape. The current icon says only "mosque".
  • The motif is much bigger: about 47 × 54 dp (x 30.5–77.5, y 25–79.5), against a small dome. No foreground ink falls outside the 66 dp safe circle. Its visual centre of mass sits at about (54, 52), slightly above the middle.
  • It has no broken parts. The current crescent path has zero area and never renders.
  • It has a real monochrome layer.
  • It uses three foreground colours, all flat fills or simple gradients, and is safe to convert to a VectorDrawable.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • 48 px: the arch-on-columns outline, the dark niche and the gold 3:00 hands read clearly. The ribs read as tapering rays that widen toward the rim, not as thin gauge ticks. The capitals show as one-pixel steps. The minute hand ends 3.5 dp below the 12 mark, and the hour hand (10 dp long) ends about 3 dp from the right capital and well clear of the 2 o'clock rib, so nothing touches.
  • 24 px: the ribs merge into texture. What remains is a light horseshoe gate with a gold L in a dark niche, which is still recognisable.

Risks

  • An arched top with a dial in it can still read as a grandfather clock or a clock tower, and the 3:00 "L" is a very clock-like pose. The capitals, the pronounced horseshoe return and the wide base reduce this, but do not remove it.

  • 3:00 matches winter Asr only approximately; in summer Asr in Tunis is closer to 16:10.
  • The hour hand on 3 puts a little more weight on the right half.
  • The clock hands carry the gold, which leaves no gold for any other accent.

Palette

At 24px it still reads as a white five-petal flower with a gold mark in the centre, but the crescent shape is no longer distinct. The petal shade turns into a soft two-tone and does not become noise. In monochrome, the five petals are solid shapes around a round hole, and the crescent is a solid shape inside that hole. The gap around the crescent replaces the teal ring, so the crescent is cut out of the flower by negative space. The asymmetric pointed petals carry the twist without colour, and the crescent stays visible in the themed icon at 48px.

Risks

The prayer-times purpose rests on one small crescent, and the "5 petals = 5 prayers" reading has to be learned. A white five-petal flower can still look like a spa or wellness app at a glance, though the crescent and the slim twisted petals now push it away from frangipani. The jasmine is also linked to the 2011 "Jasmine Revolution", which some users may read politically. That is a content question for the owner to settle before this direction goes further. The horn tips of the crescent are sharp and soften to a blur below 36px. A mechmoum-style cluster (one flower with 2-3 buds) would be more specifically Tunisian, but it would shrink the main flower and the crescent below the 48px limit, so this version keeps the single blossom.

مزولة

Sundial

The marble sundial (mizwala) of a Tunisian mosque courtyard, used to read Dhuhr and Asr from the sun. The best-known one is the 19th-century marble dial in the courtyard of the Great Mosque of Kairouan (dated 1258 AH, about 1842 CE). The icon shows a half-disc dial plate on a plinth, a rod gnomon with a ball nodus, the sun on one side and the gnomon's dark shadow lying across the plate on the other side, crossing the gold Asr curve. The prayer time is read from the sun, which is what the app does.

The weakest of the set. Refined: a sail-like blade became a thin rod gnomon with an Asr curve, but it is still busy at 48 px.

Symbolism, small sizes, risks

Symbolism

  • Ivory half-disc plate seen slightly from above, on a straight stone plinth: the engraved marble slab of a mosque sundial, lying in a courtyard.
  • Two hour lines cut into the plate (the teal background shows through them), tapering into the gnomon's foot: the fan of hour lines that every line on the dial shares. The noon line is hidden behind the rod.
  • Gold rod gnomon (shakhis), 3 dp wide, with a lit face and a shaded face, topped by a ball nodus: the vertical style of a Maghrebi horizontal dial, whose tip marks the time.
  • Dark shadow wedge from the gnomon's foot to the plate's rim along an afternoon line: the shadow is the dial's "hand" and the icon's main mark.
  • Dark-gold curve on the afternoon side, passing under the shadow: the Asr line. This makes the sundial a prayer instrument rather than a garden sundial.
  • Gold disc at upper left: the sun, on the opposite side from the shadow, so the cause and the effect are both in the picture.

Palette

  • #00796B → #004D40 background, vertical teal gradient (brand teal). It also shows through the hour lines.
  • #FFF8F0 → #EFE2C6 marble plate, cream to light gold (brand cream / light gold)
  • #D6C197 plinth front (stone, the ivory's shade facet)
  • #E2B845 the single gold: sun, ball nodus and the lit face of the rod (brand gold #D4AF37 lifted for contrast on teal)
  • #B8860B gold shade facet (brand accent): the shaded face of the rod and the Asr curve
  • #0A3B33 shadow wedge
  • That makes 5 foreground tones in 3 families (ivory, gold, ink). The edge strip and the extra hour-line colour are gone.

Why it beats the current icon

It says "prayer time from the sun" instead of the generic "mosque" that every prayer app uses, and it has no Ottoman pencil minarets or onion dome. The reference is local and historical (the Kairouan and Zitouna courtyard sundials), not a cliché. The motif is about 57 dp wide and 43 dp tall, against the current small dome. Every shape renders, unlike the zero-area crescent path in the current icon. It ships with a real monochrome layer.

Small sizes & themed icon

At 48 px four shapes read: the gold sun, the white dial on its plinth, the dark shadow wedge and the gold pin of the gnomon. The hour lines and the Asr curve stay visible as engraving. At 24 px the hour lines and the Asr curve fade into the plate. What remains is a sun dot, a white mound with a dark wedge and a thin gold pin. That still reads as a sun and a dial, though the gnomon is down to about 1 px. In the monochrome layer the plate is split by a 2 dp channel around the rod. The shadow wedge is a cut-out from the foot to the rim and the two hour lines are cut-outs. The rod stands on the solid plinth and the sun stays a solid disc, so without colour it still reads as sun + gnomon + shadow + dial. The Asr curve is left out of the monochrome layer because it would only add noise at themed-icon sizes.

Risks

  • A half-disc with a dark wedge from its centre and a curved line can read as a gauge or speedometer. The sun and the ball-topped gnomon carry the sundial reading, but at a glance the dial-and-needle reading is there.
  • Sundials are not a widely known symbol. Many users will see "sun + dial" without knowing the Kairouan mizwala, so the Tunisian specificity lives in the story more than in the picture.
  • The 3 dp rod is thin by design (faithful to a mizwala). At 24 px it is about 1 px, and its lit half is low-contrast on the ivory plate. The shaded half and the ball keep it visible.
  • The shadow's direction is stylised. The wedge widens toward the rim, which a real rod's shadow does not. The ball nodus has no separate shadow spot, and the shadow is drawn longer than the Asr point to make it the main mark.
  • It is still 5 foreground tones, more than the 3 the rules prefer. They read as 3 families, but a strict reading would drop the plinth tone or the gold shade.
  • The nodus is r 3 dp rather than the suggested 2.5 dp, so that it still reads at 48 px. Next to the r 8 sun this gives two gold discs, which are told apart only by their size.

حضن الهلال

Crescent Embrace

Tunisia's silhouette, in cream, held from the west and the south by a gold crescent on teal. A square, Kairouan-style minaret rises from the crescent's belly. Together they say "prayer, for Tunisia, and nowhere else".

The most literal Tunisia. The outline was traced from the app's own boundary data and redrawn as 24 smooth nodes, with a gold Kairouan minaret added. A national map can make the app look like an official ministry service.

Symbolism, small sizes, risks

Symbolism

  • The country. The silhouette is drawn as a designed shape with 24 smooth nodes. It is based on a trace of the app's own delegation_boundaries.json (geoBoundaries ADM2), which covers every delegation the app serves. It keeps Cap Bon, the Gulf of Tunis, the Gulf of Hammamet, the Sahel bulge at Ras Kaboudia, the Gulf of Gabès, Djerba and the Saharan tip.
  • The crescent. It stands for the Islamic frame and the lunar (hijri) calendar the app follows. It opens towards the north-east, the way the country faces the sea. Wrapped around the country, it reads as the app looking after the whole of Tunisia. Its lit side faces south-west, the usual orientation of the crescent emblem. It is not meant as the evening new moon that starts Ramadan or Eid, which in Tunisia is lit on the lower right.
  • The minaret. It is the prayer cue: the call to prayer, the times the app announces. Its three stacked square tiers and small dome follow the Great Mosque of Kairouan, so the tower is a Tunisian Maliki minaret, not a generic Ottoman pencil one. It also echoes the Kairouan touches in the sister TV app «أفق».
  • What is left out. There is no star and no red, so the icon cannot be mistaken for the flag.

Palette

  • #00695C → #004D40: background, a radial teal gradient centred slightly above the middle (the existing app teal).
  • #D4AF37: crescent and minaret, one flat gold (the app's brand gold). The minaret and crescent therefore join without a seam.
  • #FFF8F0: the country and Djerba (the app's cream).
  • #000000: the monochrome layer.

Why it beats the current icon

  • It is unmistakably Tunisian twice over: the country's shape and a Kairouan square minaret. The current dome between Ottoman pencil minarets could belong to any country, and its minarets are the wrong type for Tunisia.
  • The motif is large: the ink spans 42 × 58 dp (x 31.8–74.0, y 24.5–82.5) and the country alone is 50 dp tall. The current hero is a 28 × 42 dp dome flanked by thin minarets.
  • Every element renders. The current crescent path has zero area and never shows.
  • It has a real monochrome layer.
  • It survives every launcher mask. All ink sits inside the 66 dp safe circle: Cap Bon reaches 32.1 dp from the centre and the crescent 29.6 dp.
  • It is optically centred. The visual mass sits at (53.9, 55.1) and the ink bounding box is centred at (52.9, 53.5).
  • The outline is clean. The old 70-vertex trace showed 3–7 px nicks at 512 px. The new outline uses smooth cubic curves with no wiggle smaller than about 1.5 dp, and every coordinate has at most 2 decimals.

Small sizes & themed icon

  • Gaps. The country is at least 3.6 dp from the crescent and 3.8 dp from the minaret. Djerba is 2.3 dp from the mainland.
  • 48 px. The country is about 33 px tall. Cap Bon's hook, the Gulf of Gabès notch, Djerba as a dot and the Saharan point all read. The minaret is a clear gold tower about 4–5 px wide on the crescent. Its tier steps blur into a tapered top.
  • 24 px. It becomes a cream wedge cradled by a gold arc with a small spike. The coastline detail and the Djerba channel fill in, but the "moon, tower and land" figure is still recognisable.
  • Monochrome. The crescent and minaret merge into one flat shape, as they already do in colour, and the country stays a separate shape. Nothing depends on colour contrast, so the themed icons read as well as the colour icon.

Risks

  • It may look official. A country map can read as a state service. The icon has no flag, emblem or star and uses the app's own teal and gold. Even so, do not put «الرسمي» or similar wording next to this icon in the store listing: Google Play's policy forbids implying government affiliation. Say instead that the times come from the INM.
  • The minaret stands outside the border. It sits in the pocket west of the Algerian border, so a viewer reading it as a map could place it in Algeria. It is meant as part of the crescent emblem, not as a map pin.
  • Fine detail is lost when small. The minaret's tier steps are only 0.8–1.1 dp each, so below about 64 px it is just a tower.
  • Cartographic licence. Djerba is enlarged and given a 2.3 dp channel, Kerkennah and La Galite are omitted, small lagoons are filled, and the coast is simplified to 24 nodes.
  • It names nothing directly. At 24 px and below, the silhouette is just a blob. Someone who does not already know Tunisia's shape sees only "a crescent, a tower and a shape".
  • The template is common. Map plus crescent is a familiar layout. The Kairouan minaret helps, but the icon is still less distinctive than a constructed mark.
  • Overlap with 06. On purpose, this version does not add five prayer dots, so it stays clearly apart from 06 «هلال المواقيت», which uses a fat ivory crescent with dots. Here the crescent is thin, gold and wraps a map.
