Prayer Silence Icons

Round 2 of the launcher icon for «أوقات الصلاة تونس». Every proposal now shows the app's main job: the phone goes quiet while you pray. A prayer rug replaces the mosque architecture of round 1, and the colours come from Kairouan carpets, Sidi Bou Said blue and Nabeul clay instead of dark teal and gold.

Best first. Switch the launcher mask below to see each icon the way different phones cut it.

سجادة صامتة

Muted Rug

A Kairouan prayer rug with a mute badge, the way a video call marks a muted person.

Silence
The speaker with a × is the phone's own mute sign. It reads at once, down to 36 px.
Prayer
A fringed rug seen from above, in Sidi Bou Said blue with one Kairouan lozenge. No building and no crescent.
Watch out
Read literally, it says the rug is muted. The × stays inside the badge and never touches the rug, but you should be comfortable with that reading.

سجادة الصمت

Volume Rug

The prayer rug is the phone's volume slider, pushed all the way down to mute.

Silence
The white knob holds the mute sign at the foot of the track, like Android's volume panel at zero.
Prayer
A madder-red Kairouan rug with white wool fringe and one lozenge.
Watch out
The slider idea only lands for people who know the volume panel. Everyone else still sees a rug and a mute sign. In the themed icon the fringe can look like the pins of a chip.

هلال السكينة

Quiet Crescent

The crescent is the sign of Islam and also the Do Not Disturb icon on Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo and iPhone. Here it shelters a prayer rug.

Silence
It looks like the Do Not Disturb tile switched on. Clear for Samsung and Xiaomi users, weaker on Pixel, where Do Not Disturb is a circle with a bar.
Prayer
The crescent, and a cream rug with a madder prayer niche.
Watch out
Many people will read the crescent as the hilal first, so silence is the second reading. A crescent holding an object is a common pattern among prayer apps.

Also drawn

08

ششش

Shhh

One calligraphic ش, the letter Arabic uses to write "shh". Its teeth step down and its tail fades out like a ring dying away.

Silence
Arabic readers hear «ششش», and the fading tail helps. Nothing shows a phone.
Prayer
Only through the app's name. No religious sign is used.
Watch out
It can pass for the logo of a café, a pottery brand or a keyboard app. It is the most stylish of the four and the least literal, so it may suit a splash screen or wordmark better than the launcher.
